# M4-03 Bestiary to the content floor

Date: 2026-11-06

- **Decision:** 48 monsters (11 before, 37 new) from level 1 to 30, each a base plus overrides.
  Monsters gain `attacks` (damage amount and type, delivery, optional phase), `resist` (flat
  by damage type, the combat pipeline's `defender.resist`), and `role` (standard, boss,
  minion). Spec: `docs/modules/monsters.md#attacks-resistances-and-role`.
  **[V 2026-11-06, `python -m unittest ruleset/scripts/test_bestiary.py`]**
- **Decision:** "every phase used as an attack and as a resistance" reads the phase as
  two-sided: a monster's own `phase` is its resistance side, through the cycles, and an
  attack's `phase` is its attack side. Every one of the 13 damage types is an attack and a
  resistance of some monster. **[A]** for the reading.
- **Decision:** both monster balance checks are gates. The 11 older monsters keep their levels
  (the loot vectors read them) and come into band: HP and the strongest damage per tier, and
  the ember drake, stone golem as bosses (the golem's 110 HP at level 5 was a boss's 20 hits).
  The hits-to-kill band follows the `role`, and the damage check reads attack amounts as
  well as `damage_*` abilities. **[V 2026-11-06, `balance_report.py --gate`]**
- **Decision:** base growth caps an attribute at 10: a level-30 monster on a level-4 base
  grew past the schema's 1 to 10. `validate.py` and `monsters.md` say so. **[V 2026-11-06]**
- **Decision:** each new monster has a drop row on an existing table, within the loot budget
  at its level. **[V 2026-11-06, `test_loot.py`]**
- **Why:** 11 monsters, all below level 8, could not carry levels 1 to 30.
- **Open:** the loot tables stop at level 7, so a level-30 boss rolls the level-7 hoard two or
  three times; tables for the high tiers are a loot issue **[U]**. The combat pipeline does not
  read a monster row yet; the caller copies `attacks` and `resist` into a combat input **[U]**.
  Phase interaction on monster attacks (the wuxing multiplier) is not in the combat pipeline
  **[U]**.

## Review: families and templates (2026-11-08)

- **Decision:** a monster's base may be another monster, `core:monster/<key>`, as an
  equipment row names its base: a family is written once and its members say what differs.
  The derived monster inherits everything, abilities and core drop included, and the creature
  type stays the root's. Spec: `docs/modules/monsters.md#derived-monsters`.
  **[V 2026-11-08, `python -m unittest ruleset/scripts/test_bestiary.py`]**
- **Decision:** a derived monster states its `level` in `overrides`. The Go loader and
  `loot_reference.py` read a level from the row or its creature type, so they need no chain
  walk. **[V 2026-11-08, `go test ./...`, `test_loot.py`]**
- **Decision:** five element templates, one per phase (fire, frost → water, storm → metal,
  acid → earth, venom → wood), in `monsters/templates.json`. A template sets the phase, turns
  every attack into its damage type, adds resistance, appends tags, and prefixes the label;
  overrides still win. Element variants are a base monster plus a template: the acid, fire,
  and frost oozes, the frost giant, and the storm giant youth. **[V 2026-11-08]**
- **Decision:** 13 rows became derived monsters. Each resolves to what it did before except
  where the family adds something: tags from a template; the parent's abilities and core drop
  (the bandit captain's leg sweep, the ancient dragon's fiery breath, the golems' stun); the
  acid ooze's attributes from the new `ooze` type (it was a `plant`); the frost giant's maul,
  now frost. Four rows are new: ooze, fire ooze, frost ooze, giant. All monster gates pass.
  **[V 2026-11-08, `balance_report.py --gate`]**
- **Decision:** names come from folklore, myth, or plain description, never from another
  game's product identity; `test_bestiary.py` fails on a list of protected names (beholder,
  mind flayer, flan, and others). **[V 2026-11-08]**
- **Difference from the vector:** the vector commit listed the ice elemental as a fire
  elemental with the frost template. Built that way it overrode nearly every field (abilities,
  resistances, core drop), so it is not a family member; it stays a row on the `elemental`
  type, and the test no longer lists it.
- **Open:** inherited core drops keep the parent's grades (a level-30 ancient dragon drops a
  lesser to common core, as the ember drake does); core grades by level are a later pass
  **[U]**. The thunderbird is a sacred figure in several Indigenous traditions; not a
  copyright risk, but a rename (storm roc, tempest eagle) may be kinder **[A]**. Treant is a
  coined fantasy word, free to use (it is in the CC BY 4.0 SRD 5.1), not a franchise name
  **[A]**.
