# M3-02 Spell list to the content floor

Date: 2026-11-08

- **Decision:** 70 spells, 5 to 9 per color, from level 1 to 30 (8 before, 62 new). An
  authored spell draws only effects on its own color or with no color. Every color has a
  damage, a utility, and a defensive spell except two documented gaps: red has no utility
  spell (it is the offense school; its range is shape: bolt, burst, storm), and green deals
  no damage (restoration does not harm; life taken is black's). Spec:
  `docs/modules/magic.md#the-core-spell-list`.
  **[V 2026-11-08, `python -m unittest ruleset/scripts/test_spells.py`]**
- **Decision:** five damage effects join the pool, one per damage type the spell side lacked,
  each on a fitting color: `damage_radiant` (white), `damage_necrotic` (black),
  `damage_psychic` (yellow), `damage_thunder` (indigo), `damage_acid` (blue). `summon` loses
  its default color (black), since conjuration (orange) and necromancy (black) both summon.
  Seven reserved effects are now used and lose the flag (`damage_shock`, `light`,
  `telekinesis`, `bound_weapon`, `fortify_defense`, `drain_resource`, `drain_attribute`).
  **[V 2026-11-08, `validate.py`]**
- **Decision:** hazards gain `nature` (`mechanical`, `natural`, `arcane`), not a second
  `kind`, since `kind` (trap, environment) was taken; the level was already there. An arcane
  hazard names a `color` and no check row for detect and disarm: both roll `<color>_magic`.
  The fire rune is red and the lightning ward violet. Spec: `docs/core/hazards.md#nature`.
  **[V 2026-11-08, hazard vectors in Python and Go]**
- **Decision:** the hazard command's `op: spell` applies a cast that succeeded:
  `disarm_hazard` N undoes a mechanical trap of level N or below, `dispel` N an arcane hazard
  of level N or below; nothing undoes a natural hazard. New spells: Disarm Trap (4), Greater
  Disarm (20), Unlock (2), Greater Unlock (15), Dispel Ward (12, area), Greater Dispel (25).
  **[V 2026-11-08]**
- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-08):** a spell's cost comes from its effects alone, never the
  caster's level: per effect `0.1 × baseCost × magnitude × duration`, plus `0.05 × baseCost ×
  radius` for an area, times the target multiplier (self and touch 1, ranged 1.5, area 2);
  the spell cost is the sum, rounded up. A magnitude in seconds, or with no unit, is not a
  strength and counts as 1, so control is priced by its duration. Every spell effect carries
  a `baseCost`; the coefficients are `spell_cost` in `casting/core.json`. Every spell, the
  eight older ones too, now costs what the formula gives. Spec:
  `docs/modules/magic.md#cost-formula`. **[V 2026-11-08, `test_spells.py`, casting vectors in
  Python and Go]**
- **Decision:** the shape is Morrowind's and the area multiplier Daggerfall's: OpenMW's
  `calcEffectCost` (mean magnitude × 0.1 × base cost × duration, plus 0.05 × area × base
  cost, × 1.5 on a target; an effect with no magnitude counts 1) at OpenMW/openmw
  `b8285cc95d19`; Daggerfall Unity's `ApplyTargetCostMultiplier` (caster and touch 1.0, at
  range 1.5, area around the caster 2.0, area at range 2.5) and its skill discount at
  Interkarma/daggerfall-unity `4e78e1412b17`; Morrowind's base-cost ratios from UESP's
  Morrowind:Magic Effects (Light 0.2, Fire Damage 5, Damage Health 8, Paralyze and Silence 40),
  scaled to our magnitudes. Not taken: Daggerfall's magnitude and duration that grow per
  caster level, Morrowind's global cost multiplier and its one-second duration offset.
  **[V 2026-11-08, read in the downloaded sources]**
- **Decision:** six spells were retuned because the formula priced what they did: Renewal
  healed 300 per ally (now 6 a second for 15 seconds), Petrify held 30 seconds (now 10),
  Stoneskin and Bone Armor 60 seconds (now 30), Purity Ward 120 (now 60), Silence 10 (now 6);
  Summon Familiar holds 60 seconds; Ice Storm, Inferno, and Mass Charm were raised so they
  outrank weaker spells. Every spell is affordable at its level for a caster with INT 8
  (`INT × 8 + level × 2`). **[A]** for the budget.
- **Decision:** a crafted spell carries no save; saves are for authored instant spells
  (M3-05). The caster's side, the skill discount and perks on `spell_cost`, is M3-07; until
  then a cast spends `cost.mana` as authored. **[A]**
- **Difference from the vector:** the first vector priced new spells with §5's old formula
  (unit weights: 6 per level, 0.25 per second). It gave a 1-mana paralyze, a 4-mana petrify,
  a 42-mana feather fall, a 141-mana greater dispel, and a 3-mana regeneration of 40 HP, since
  it counted an over-time magnitude once. A level rule replaced it, then, on the owner's
  review, the effect formula above; the casting vectors now spend the new costs (fire bolt 6,
  was 5). Two older hazard vectors that detected the fire rune with
  investigation (difficulty and a lantern's modifier) moved to the poison needle, a
  mechanical trap, because an arcane rune is no longer found with investigation.
- **Why:** 8 spells could not carry nine schools over 30 levels, and traps could only be
  handled with lockpick.
- **Open:** the base costs are a first calibration against our magnitudes **[A]**; long
  buffs stay linear in duration, as in Morrowind, so a very long buff is dear. Locks are not rows: Unlock emits its level for
  the consumer to compare **[A]**. Area dispel reaches each arcane hazard in its radius by one
  call each; the caller finds them **[A]**. Spell magnitudes are not balance-gated; a spell
  band next to the monster bands is open **[U]**.
- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-09):** `docs/modules/magic.md#the-nine-color-schools` defines
  what each school does, with its spells and the line between overlapping schools. It
  describes the data as it stands. **[V 2026-11-09]**
- **Open:** three skill descriptions in `skills/core.json` disagree with the spell list:
  red names drains (they are violet's and black's), blue names locks (Unlock is indigo's),
  and red names poison (no spell has it). **[V 2026-11-09]**
