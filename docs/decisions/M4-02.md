# M4-02 Loot and treasure tables

Date: 2026-11-06

- **Decision:** `ruleset/data/loot/core.json` holds d100 loot tables (coins, an item ref, or
  nothing per range), one drop row per core monster, and `budget_cp = 500 * level * level` as
  a formula tree. Items are valued at list price through the price formula, so loot and prices
  are one scale. A table's expected value must fit the budget at its level;
  `test_loot.py` checks every table. Spec: `docs/core/loot.md`. **[V 2026-11-04]**
- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-06):** a monster's drop row is a list of loot rows, not one table.
  A row is a table (optional `rolls` and `shift`) or an item (optional `quantity`); either may
  take a d100 roll-under `chance` and a `material`. Entries go from least to most valuable, so a
  shift skips the bottom results. A monster's expected value must fit the budget at its own
  level. 29 seeded vectors; `test_loot.py` checks the entry order and every monster's budget.
  **[V 2026-11-06]**
- **Why:** no loot data existed; monsters had no drops and levels no treasure budget. With one
  table per monster, monsters that share a table (skeleton and zombie, dire wolf and grove
  stalker) could not differ in rates, extras, or quality without copying the table. Generic
  tiers would lose the theme; table inheritance breaks the 1-to-100 coverage when the parent
  changes; weights lose the printed d100 chart.
- **Open:** the budget curve (5 gp at level 1, 2000 gp at level 20) is a proposal **[A]**.
  Drops live in the loot file, not on monster rows, so a module can change a monster's loot
  without touching the bestiary **[A]**. Breakthrough cores (`coreDrop`) stay with the
  breakthrough module; it can add its own tables and loot rows with `chance`, so a wolf and a
  dire wolf differ by data alone **[A]**. Only weapons and armor take a row's material **[A]**.
  The looter's own shift (a perk or Luck on loot rolls) and nested tables are left for later
  **[U]**. Magic items, unique items, and campaign wealth curves are not covered **[U]**.
