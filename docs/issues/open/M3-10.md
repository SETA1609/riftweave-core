#### M3-10 Power and nature templates for summons and monsters
- Status: todo
- Priority: medium
- Needs: M3-08, M3-12
- Do: Summons stay static and uncraftable: a spell names rows, a row appears at the caster's level, and only a GM or dev adds a new type. A row sets the stats, so a summoned dog stays weaker than a summoned wolf at the same level, whatever is added on top. Templates gain a `slot`: `element` (the five that exist), `power` (`dire`, `ancient`, `elder`, and so on), and `nature` (`demonic`, `angelic`, `otherworldly`, and so on), applied in that order. `phase` and `damage_type` become optional, and a template may carry attribute adds, resistances, tags, and a spell list. A monster's attributes have no maximum: 10 is only a player's cap at character creation, and perks or bonuses can pass it. Remove `maximum: 10` from the monster schema and the upper clamp from level growth; the minimum of 1 stays. A monster has one template per slot. Power is a ladder of perks: buying `elder` needs the `dire` perk (ordinary perk prerequisites), and the latest bought replaces the one before. Nature is kept simple here: the latest bought replaces the one before, and only it is tracked; whether a game locks it as a one-time choice is left for later. The caster's current power and nature apply when the creature is summoned, through the same merge as a row's templates, so no row is written per combination. A perk names what it unlocks in a new `unlocks` list of refs, because a modifier holds only a number; M3-09 can use the same field for a bound weapon's material. A template never changes the creature type: an undead horse has the `undead` base, not `beast`, so there is no `undead` template, and `dire` is not applied twice. Monster stats read as a player's do: a template's attribute adds move HP and mana by the derived rows' `per_point` (END +2 is +16 HP) [A], and a creature with a spell list gets mana from the `mp` row. Monster rows gain a reduced skill sheet, `skills`: only the skills the creature fights or casts with are written (its weapon skill, `evasion`, `block`, a `<color>_magic`), and any other skill reads as a player's starting value, `5 + associatedAbility × 2`. Existing rows keep their authored HP. The same merge generates monsters: any row plus a power and a nature is a monster, with no row written for the combination, so no one has to author an `ancient_otherworldly_dragon`. A generated monster is named by its row ref, `power`, and `nature` [A]. Authored rows can use the new templates too, and only rows kept as examples are written out: `dire_wolf` becomes a `wolf` row with `templates: ["dire"]`. Keep some written-out rows as examples: at least a dog and a wolf at the same level, a dire and an elder wolf, a demonic hound, and an undead horse. An example's HP and mana come from the player formulas with average rolls: the derived function plus `(level − 1) × ⌊LCK / 2⌋ / 2` growth, rounded down; check them against the balance bands. Fix the stale seed formula in `skill.schema.json`. Summon groups are not split by tier: the caster's level does that job (M3-08). Attack damage grows with level: each creature type's `growth` gains `damagePerLevel` (0.25 for all of them), added per level above the row's level and rounded, on attack damage and `damage_*` ability magnitudes, as `hitPointsPerLevel` does for HP. A monster uses the attribute modifier of M3-12 (`attribute − 5`), with no upper limit. What an action does decides which attribute applies, and a monster's attack depends on how it is defined: an attack names its attribute in `ability`, so a jaguar's claw uses `agi` (like finesse) and an elephant's stomp uses `str`. A monster's attack rolls dice, as a weapon does, and `damage.amount` goes. A new `natural_weapons` collection (`core:natural_weapon/<key>`: bite, claw, gore, slam, talons, sting, and so on) gives each natural attack its dice. Natural weapons are not equipment: they cannot be bought, looted, or enchanted. An attack names one natural weapon, or for a humanoid a carried weapon (`core:weapon/<key>`), and rolls that row's dice plus the attribute modifier. The attack may still set its damage type, and a row's overrides win over a template's. An elemental has a physical attack, a natural weapon that deals physical damage, plus two spell-like abilities of its element: a bolt (target `ranged`) and a touch (target `touch`). A natural weapon's dice move along the dice ladder with creature size (tiny −2, small −1, medium 0, large +1, huge +2) [A]. `ability` defaults to `str`. An ooze has no weapon: it fights with a spell-like ability, a touch-target elemental effect, so its four rows lose `pseudopod` and the rule that every monster has `attacks` accepts a damaging ability instead. Other attacks that are neither natural nor carried, such as a breath or a bolt, become spell-like abilities too [A]. A spell-like ability takes the modifier only if it names an attribute [A]. A spell-like ability pays as a cast does: mana from the spell cost formula on its effects, and at a table the `cast` action's AP. A creature whose only attack is a spell-like ability, such as an ooze, pays the AP and no mana, so it cannot run out of attacks [A]. The elemental base gains `int` (5, growing 0.4 per level [A]): a missing attribute reads 0, so a level-5 elemental has 10 mana today, one bolt. A venom elemental's bolt and touch need a new spell-channel `damage_poison`, because `poison` has only the poison and coating channels. Rows whose dice and modifier land below their tier's damage band raise their attributes, now uncapped, or their dice. `balance_report.py` reads a monster's damage as the dice mean plus the modifier. Check `damagePerLevel` against the damage bands once every row has its dice [U].
