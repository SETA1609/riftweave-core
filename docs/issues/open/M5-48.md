#### M5-48 Balance checks for potions, magic, and scrolls
- Status: todo
- Priority: medium
- Needs: M2-09, M3-06
- Do: Owner rules, 2026-11-12. Make them balance checks in `ruleset/data/balance/core.json`, beside the weapon and monster checks, so the data cannot drift from them:
  1. **A stock potion is weaker than magic doing the same task.** A stock potion is one that is bought, looted, or authored; it is not made by a character. Today it fails at low skill. At green magic 0, a Potion of Mana (15 mana, 20 toxicity) spent on Mend Wounds (12 HP for 6 mana) heals 1.5 HP per toxicity point. Potions of Minor Healing and Healing heal 2.0, and Greater Healing 2.3. The two only break even at green magic 50, where the cast discount (skill / 200, at most 45%) applies. The measure [V owner 2026-11-12]: at a magic skill of 50, the spell route (a Potion of Mana spent on the matching spell) must beat every stock potion for the same task, per toxicity point. Today they only tie at 50, so the lever is still needed: weaker stock healing potions, a stronger or less toxic Potion of Mana, or both [A]. Whether gold per HP is checked too is open [A].
  2. **Instant costs more toxicity than over time.** An instant X costs more toxicity than N over time that totals X. Toxicity is both [V owner 2026-11-12]. A formula of magnitude and duration, in data, defines it and gives instant a higher toxicity than over time. A stock potion, and a TTRPG table, keeps a static `toxicity` number on the row, set at or near what the formula gives for that row. The check computes the formula for each stock row and fails when the static number strays from it by more than a tolerance [A: tolerance and rounding]. Crafted potions (the alchemy module) use the formula directly.
  3. **Scrolls cost only gold.** A scroll has no toxicity and no limit on use. Its price is the only check on it, so the check compares a scroll's price with the cost of the spell it casts [A].
  - **Later, in the post-1.0 modules:** crafted potions may beat stock potions. Refined ingredients raise magnitudes, and one brew can stack effects, such as an instant heal plus healing over time, for about a stock potion's toxicity. Perks can lower toxicity or double a brew's output, so a dedicated alchemist can match a niche mage. Magic items give spell-like abilities with casts or uses per day. This issue only keeps the stock rows honest so those systems have a baseline to beat.
